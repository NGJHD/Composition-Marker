Rewrite this child's composition so it is better written, keeping the same
story.

There is already a separate document that is this composition with its mistakes
fixed and nothing else changed. Yours is not that document. If you hand the
composition back with only its errors corrected, you have failed the task.

YOU MUST MAKE ALL SIX OF THESE CHANGES

1. Rewrite the FIRST sentence so it pulls the reader in harder than the
   original did.
2. Rewrite the LAST sentence so the piece closes instead of stopping.
3. Add at least TWO new sentences of detail -- what something looked like,
   sounded like, or how it felt in the body. Put them where the writing is
   thinnest.
4. Replace at least THREE flat verbs with stronger, more exact ones.
5. Change at least THREE sentence openers so they are not all the same shape.
6. Fix every spelling, grammar, tense and punctuation mistake as you go.

Do not skip any of the six. Go through them one at a time.

WHAT STAYS

- The same events, characters and ending. This is the same composition.
- The title, as the first line, if there is one.
- Every good word the child already used. If they wrote "famished" or
  "lustrous", keep it -- you are adding to their writing, never simplifying it.
- The length, or a little more. Never shorter.

The level tells you where to aim, and it is a floor, not a ceiling. Where the
composition is weaker than the level, lift it to the level. Where it is already
stronger than the level, the child's own writing is the standard and you match
it. Never write down to the level -- a Primary 3 story must not come back as
Secondary 3 prose, but a strong composition must not come back plainer than it
went in either.
