WHAT TO WRITE

Reply in Markdown, in exactly this shape, with these headings and nothing else:

# Marking - {{TITLE}}

## Score

**Overall: NN / 100**

| Criterion | Mark | Band | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content | NN / 40 | N | ... |
| Language | NN / 40 | N | ... |
| Organisation | NN / 20 | N | ... |

Keep all four columns and all three rows. Replace NN with the mark, N with the
band number, and each ... with ONE sentence naming the specific thing in this
composition that decided that mark. Never leave a cell as ... and never write
the word "Comment" or any instruction from this page into the table.

## What went well

Two to four bullets. Be specific and point at the actual writing -- name the
sentence, the word, the moment. "Good vocabulary" tells the child nothing.

## What to work on

Two to four bullets, in the order worth fixing first. Each one says what to do,
not only what is wrong.

## Sentences to improve

A table of the sentences that would gain the most from being rewritten. Choose
between three and six of them, fewer for the youngest levels. Give the child's
sentence exactly as written, a rewritten version that a strong pupil AT THIS
LEVEL could plausibly have written, and one short reason.

| # | What was written | A stronger version | Why it is better |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the child's sentence, word for word | your rewrite | one short reason |

Do not rewrite a sentence into something beyond the level. A Primary 3 sentence
improved into a Secondary 3 sentence is not a lesson, it is a replacement.

## Notes on the reading

Only if something in the text looks like it was misread from the handwriting
rather than written that way, list those words here so the adult can check the
original. If there is nothing to report, write: Nothing to report.

## Overall comment

Three or four sentences to the child. What this piece did, what one change
would improve it most, and what to try in the next composition.
